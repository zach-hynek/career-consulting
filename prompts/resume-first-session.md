# Resume-first builder session

Use the existing Career Studio and Job Board applications to improve my actual resume and develop the standard intake workflow. Read `AGENTS.md`, `docs/resume-first-workflow.md`, `skills/career-resume-review/SKILL.md`, `apps/career-studio/docs/editor-agent.md`, and `apps/job-board/docs/tailoring-studio.md` first.

Start with my existing resume upload or my own LinkedIn profile/export. Parse it into a visible structured preview, preserve its source and identify ambiguities. Then conduct a short interview—two or three relevant questions at a time—to uncover my personal contributions, systems, scope and outcomes. Accept rough voice-to-text answers. Do not invent metrics or treat hypothetical examples as achievements.

Turn confirmed answers into a reusable private fact library with provenance and candidate-approved bullet variants. Keep that library separate from any one-page or two-page application. Let me select, swap and reword bullets without losing the full career history.

For a specific job, explain the fit and gaps, then suggest multiple exact edit locations with before/after wording, supporting confirmed fact IDs and the requirement addressed. Preserve the template's design and page budget. Use job-posting language only where it accurately describes my experience. Emphasize clarity, specificity and visual quality; do not claim to beat every screening system.

Use the existing revision/exact-before proposal contracts. Let me review individual edits and final materials. Keep source files and application data out of the public repository. Do not submit applications or send outreach. Improve the UX where the real walkthrough reveals friction, rather than starting with an unrelated redesign. Follow the weekly testing policy and document the resulting standard workflow.

Begin by locating the existing private resume sources, preparing the parsing preview, and asking the first focused interview questions. Stop for my answers before creating new claims or approving edits on my behalf.
