## The thesis

For Zach’s interests, I would concentrate on **science businesses that remove an expensive bottleneck for a real buyer**: electricity, grid equipment, nuclear fuel, industrial heat, semiconductor production, scientific instruments, usable AI infrastructure and enterprise deployment. The attractive employer is the organization where that demand translates into a funded team, a credible manager, useful learning and a life the candidate wants to live. A successful product or valuable stock is only one part of that judgment.

Energy is a strong starting point: the IEA describes expanding energy employment while also noting uneven conditions across technologies. That supports investigating power and grid constraints; it does not establish that every fusion startup or solar manufacturer is hiring safely. [IEA employment review](https://www.iea.org/reports/world-energy-employment-2025/executive-summary)

AI belongs in the thesis, but “AI companies will keep doing well” is too broad. Capital, adoption, margins and employment can move differently. I prefer infrastructure with repeat customers and applications with identifiable budget owners, while keeping a smaller set of frontier-model opportunities. AI’s labor effects also differ across occupations and career stages. [Stanford AI Index](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index), [Stanford labor research](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/)

My proposed search portfolio is 40% established operating businesses, 35% growth companies with a concrete commercialization path, and 25% frontier bets. This is an allocation of networking effort, not an investment recommendation or a measured optimum. Apply a separate remote/flexibility filter across all three. Someone prioritizing predictable income should shift effort toward established businesses and away from precommercial technologies.

## Where I would start

**Power and physical bottlenecks:** Schneider Electric, Eaton, Constellation Energy, BWX Technologies, Hitachi Energy and Quanta Services. These offer ways into the energy buildout beyond joining a precommercial reactor company. TerraPower, Fervo, Form Energy, Antora and Electra add more concentrated technical and execution exposure. The rationale is sector and role fit; it is not evidence that their managers are uniformly good.

**Established science and technical products:** NVIDIA, Texas Instruments, Cadence, KLA, Trane, Danaher, Vertex, Regeneron, Intuitive Surgical and ResMed. Their products create plausible paths into technical-commercial work. Public disclosure makes diligence easier, but the exact division and team still determine working conditions.

**AI commercialization and infrastructure:** Anthropic, OpenAI, Databricks, Cohere, Glean, Fireworks, Baseten and LangChain. Investigate the buyer, renewal behavior, concentration and whether the role builds transferable expertise. For Zach’s energy/AI intersection, Crusoe is particularly relevant, with project concentration and execution risk made explicit.

**Flexibility benchmarks:** Buffer, DuckDuckGo, Doist, Grafana, Supabase, Temporal, Weaviate and Bitwarden. These are valuable comparisons when deciding how much flexibility to trade for compensation or mission. Small teams may have few openings; remote-first does not establish low hours or a supportive manager.

The atlas’s “Start here,” “Specialist / growth shortlist,” and “Conditional shortlist” bands describe research order. They are not safety grades. The 25 “Start here” employers are a balanced starting slate, not a claim that they outrank every other company on pay or culture. ## How to identify employers people actually want to return to

The best public signals are measurable behavior with a clear population and time period: voluntary retention, internal promotions, referrals that become successful hires, and voluntary returners who stay. None should be reduced to one unqualified culture score.

NVIDIA reports 3.7% overall turnover for FY2026 and supplies some service-length bands in FY2025. Texas Instruments reports 10.1% overall and 7.9% voluntary turnover for 2025. DuckDuckGo publishes an employer retention claim above 95% averaged since 2012, without a fully comparable audited definition. These are useful leads with different scopes, not a clean ranking. [NVIDIA FY2026](https://images.nvidia.com/aem-dam/Solutions/documents/NVIDIA-Sustainability-Report-Fiscal-Year-2026.pdf), [NVIDIA FY2025](https://images.nvidia.com/aem-dam/Solutions/documents/NVIDIA-Sustainability-Report-Fiscal-Year-2025.pdf), [TI report](https://investor.ti.com/static-files/fc9d9346-cf77-40db-902a-e9961e9c5736), [DuckDuckGo hiring](https://duckduckgo.com/hiring)

No reliable, comparable employer-level boomerang rate was established for this screen. A return after a layoff can reflect a mistaken reduction rather than employee enthusiasm. Public success stories are selected examples, not rates. The BLS tenure measure is economy-wide and affected by age and occupation; it cannot fill missing company data. [Visier analysis](https://www.visier.com/blog/true-cost-layoff-boomerangs/), [BLS tenure](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/tenure.htm)

Additional disclosures strengthen parts of the shortlist: Cadence’s 2025 impact report gives **6.3 years average tenure** and **6.4% voluntary turnover**; KLA’s FY2026 filing gives voluntary turnover **below 3.4%**; Regeneron reports **7.4% turnover in 2025**. Eaton’s **86% engagement** measure is a survey result, not retention. These observations have different definitions, populations and periods. ResMed’s linked turnover observation is explicitly FY2025; Microsoft’s linked boomerang story is explicitly historical, from 2020. Neither is presented as a current comparable rate. [Cadence impact report](https://www.cadence.com/content/dam/cadence-www/global/en_US/documents/company/2025-cadence-corporate-impact-report.pdf), [KLA FY2026 filing](https://ir.kla.com/sec-filings/annual-reports/content/0000319201-26-000027/klac-20260630.htm), [Regeneron proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/872589/000130817926000340/regn-20260424.htm), [Eaton report](https://www.eaton.com/us/en-us/company/investor-relations/investor-toolkit/financial-reports/annual-report/letter-to-shareholders.html)

For a serious target, request cohort-level information: voluntary exits during the last 12 months divided by average team headcount; promotions divided by eligible staff; voluntary former employees rehired divided by eligible former employees; and the percentage of those returners still present after 12 months. Report laid-off returners separately. Record period, population, contractor inclusion and methodology. Raw average tenure can be diluted by rapid hiring or inflated by layoffs of newer workers. No public-profile scraping is required or recommended.

Then speak, with consent, to two current and two former people in the same function or adjacent team. Ask the same concrete questions: why the last three people left, what happened after a missed goal, whether promotions were internal, whether a respected colleague would return, and whether time off can actually be used. Treat any four conversations as qualitative evidence with selection bias. Do not claim that four people measure the whole employer.

Review sites are useful for generating questions and spotting repeated, dated patterns by role and location. Employer-sponsored awards, anonymous extremes and an overall star average receive little weight. The actual manager, recent team exits and written offer terms receive much more.

## Pay, benefits and the city where the candidate will live

The salary comparison unit must be the **same role family, seniority, geography and period**. Public jobs provide advertised ranges, not what all employees earn. This research includes five illustrative current posting/page examples, with ambiguous fields preserved. It does not manufacture an employer-wide compensation ranking from a handful of jobs.

Separate guaranteed base, target variable pay, actual quota attainment, sign-on repayment, liquid public equity and illiquid private options. Budget recurring living costs against guaranteed cash; private equity contributes zero guaranteed cash. Compare at least several genuinely matched openings before claiming a premium or discount. If the sample is small, show the sample size and mark it insufficient.

For example, the reviewed Tailscale enterprise-sales range is OTE, while the Help Scout BDR example separates base and OTE. The Glean example’s compensation labeling warrants recruiter clarification and is excluded from base-pay comparisons. The TerraPower licensing example is a senior specialist role, not a general salary benchmark. Their exact links and cautions are in the atlas.

Benefits should become dollars and usable time where possible: dependent premium contributions, deductible/out-of-pocket exposure, retirement match and vesting, parental leave eligibility, actual PTO use, disability coverage, travel, overtime/on-call and repayment clauses. Buffer’s public salary/time-off approach is useful evidence of transparency. Electra and Brimstone disclose healthcare contributions that warrant close comparison with an actual offer. Employer statements still need current benefit documents and jurisdiction checks. [Buffer time off](https://buffer.com/timeoff), [Electra careers](https://www.electra.earth/careers/), [Brimstone careers](https://www.brimstone.com/careers)

The calculator includes 18 US metros using **BEA 2024 all-items Regional Price Parities, released in 2026**, with US = 100. A nominal $150,000 salary divided by an index of 115.613/100 gives about $129,743 in national-price-level equivalents; an index of 98.629 gives about $152,085. This is a historical pretax comparison, not take-home pay, 2026 rent, a personal household budget, or a recommendation to relocate. Housing, childcare, taxes, commuting and household circumstances can dominate the difference. [BEA methodology/data](https://www.bea.gov/data/prices-inflation/regional-price-parities-state-and-metro-area), [FRED metro table](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/release/tables?eid=233895&rid=403&snid=234317)

## Remote work is a contract-level question

Record the permitted country and state, payroll entity, visa/work-right rules, office radius, required weekdays, core hours, offsites, field travel and who pays. Keep company policy separate from the job’s actual requirement. A remote role in EMEA does not become US-eligible because the company also hires Americans.

The reviewed Vercel engineering posting requires Monday, Tuesday and Friday office days near designated hubs while allowing remote work outside the commuting radius. Glean describes a four-office-day default for most teams with exceptions. Tailscale notes that some sales teams attend offices even though much of the company works remotely. These differences matter directly to a commercial candidate. [Vercel role](https://vercel.com/careers/software-engineer-accounts-5430088004), [Glean careers](https://www.glean.com/careers), [Tailscale careers](https://tailscale.com/careers)

## A candidate-controlled decision framework

Use hard constraints before ranking: legal work eligibility; minimum guaranteed base; acceptable commute and travel; required flexibility; mission/ethics; and technical qualifications. Unknown does not pass a hard constraint. It remains a question until resolved.

Proposed weights, editable in the review workspace:

| Dimension | Weight | Evidence needed |
|---|---:|---|
| Team quality, retention and employee treatment | 25 | Team exits, manager references, comparable retention, credible return stories |
| Role fit and learning | 20 | Actual responsibilities, transferable skills, decision ownership, advancement |
| Business durability and funded role | 20 | Current results or cash/runway evidence, customer concentration, approved headcount |
| Guaranteed compensation and benefits | 15 | Written base/bonus/equity and current benefit documents; matched-role comparison |
| Workplace flexibility and travel | 15 | Written job-specific policy and real practice |
| Location economics | 5 | Household budget, taxes, housing, commuting and appropriate official price data |

These weights are a proposal, not research findings. The October 30 interface adds a private A–D **personal fit grade** with a complete [machine-readable rubric](scoring-rubric.json). It does not seed company scores from funding, fame, ownership, employee-review averages or editorial priority. All employers begin unrated.

### How the A–D grade works

Each criterion has five explicit anchors: **0 — Stop signal; 1 — Weak fit; 2 — Meets my needs; 3 — Strong fit; 4 — Standout.** The scorebook and each employer’s expandable criterion show the detailed anchors, the evidence to look for, and relevant research leads. Ratings refer to a named role, team or target opportunity. A company can be a strong business and a poor personal fit.

Normalize the candidate’s nonnegative weights to 100. For each known criterion, `points = normalized weight × rating / 4`. The lower bound is the sum of earned points; the upper bound adds the full weight of unknown criteria. Missing criteria are never zero ratings and their weight is never redistributed onto known criteria. All-zero weights yield no grade.

| Letter | Points | Meaning for this candidate |
|---|---:|---|
| A | 85–100 | Front of the queue |
| B | 70 to below 85 | Worth a conversation |
| C | 50 to below 70 | Tradeoffs to resolve |
| D | Below 50 | Not my fit right now |

A range is shown when unknown inputs span multiple bands. For example, six 3/4 ratings give 75/100, or B. Leaving only location (5%) unknown produces 71.25–76.25, still B. Leaving people (25%) unknown too produces 52.5–82.5, a B–C range. Any early letter remains explicitly provisional until all support requirements are met:

- At least 70% of weighted criteria have a recorded rationale and self-reported supported or corroborated evidence.
- People, role and business each have a supported rating and rationale, even if their weights were set to zero.
- The role/team is named and hard constraints are explicitly checked as clear. A failed constraint blocks advancement, regardless of points; unknown does not pass.
- Linked workforce/project context has been considered and its relevance to this team recorded.
- Both ends of the score range fall within the same letter band.

Support levels are **first impression**, **supported**, and **corroborated**. A source can be a public URL, written role/offer terms or a private conversation recorded with date and scope. Non-empty reasoning is required for the latter two to count toward supported coverage, but the application cannot verify that reasoning. These are personal, self-reported judgments, not independent employer certifications. A workforce flag prompts diligence and is not deducted a second time automatically. Famous investors and a new funding round do not establish employee treatment or a funded role.

The six detailed anchor sets are in `scoring-rubric.json`, embedded in the page, and used directly by the rating interface. `score-engine.js` contains the same transparent arithmetic used by cards, dossiers, sorting and private CSV exports. Sorting by supported grade leaves provisional/unrated entries after supported ones; ties use editorial reading order. Existing decisions, notes, weights and constraints are preserved under the same browser storage key. Review JSON schema v2 adds criterion ratings, rationale, evidence basis, opportunity scope, constraint status, event context, update time and rubric version.

### Logos, privacy and portability

Company icon assets and provenance are stored in `logos.json` and embedded in the HTML. The page makes no live logo requests, uses no paid logo API, and remains usable offline. Icons are identification marks, not endorsements; their intellectual-property rights remain with their owners. The dossier links the asset source and retrieval date. Official-site favicons, cached website icons and employer-supplied images may differ from full marketing wordmarks. `fetch_logos.py` is a separate, manual asset-maintenance command; normal builds do not use the network. A missing or unreadable image falls back to employer initials.

The research cutoff stays October 29, 2026. This interface update does not imply new verification of all employer facts or approval of any company feed.

For startups, a recent disclosed round and credible investors earn an interview question, not a culture or solvency pass. Identify whether a transaction is primary equity, debt, secondary liquidity, a commitment or an initial closing. Ask about unrestricted cash, burn, revenue quality, the next financed milestone, option terms and customer references. Contract ceilings and backlog also differ from guaranteed revenue. The atlas preserves those distinctions in its linked capital records.

## Research coverage and limits

The screen used employer career pages, targeted workforce/business searches, filings, company announcements, official statistics and selected independent reporting. It includes 322 employer candidates, 100 expanded editorial dossiers, 90 linked company evidence notes, 60 explicit workplace-policy notes, five role-pay examples and 18 metro price entries. Initial career-page attempts and targeted repairs produced substantive page reads for 281 candidate records; blocked, thin and unreadable pages remain marked. A page read is not proof of current financial health, verified benefits for every employee, or a successful job-feed integration.

The 143 search batches are logged separately from evidence. Discovery results included namesakes, stale articles and unrelated ATS listings; those were not automatically attached to employers. No claim rests on an AI summary of a private employee profile. LinkedIn and Built In can be useful discovery references, but they are not the authority for employer-wide retention or a promise of extractable data. No login automation, blocker bypass, proxy rotation or runtime LLM pipeline was used.

Deeper coverage is concentrated in the overall Top 100 and selected workforce/flexibility examples. Collection-only entries are source-screened candidates, not 315 equally exhaustive employer audits. No complete layoff history, comparative salary survey, employee-pride survey or verified boomerang dataset is claimed. That limitation is part of the deliverable, rather than being replaced by invented scores.
